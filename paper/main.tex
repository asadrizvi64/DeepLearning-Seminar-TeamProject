\title{Do Gaussian Splats Keep the Nuclei?\\An Object-Level Audit of Gaussian Volume Compression for Fluorescence Microscopy}

\begin{abstract}
Gaussian splats are being adopted to store and stream large 3D microscopy and medical
volumes, with budgets chosen and quality reported by PSNR. We ask whether, at a given number
of bytes on disk, standard analysis still finds the nuclei. On four densely annotated frames
from two \emph{C.~elegans} embryos (\NlabTotal{} labelled nuclei), we compare a Gaussian-splat
compressor (Luxar) with JPEG-XL, JPEG2000, ZFP and downsampling at matched stored bytes,
scoring a blob detector and a learned segmenter (Cellpose) against human labels, under
decision rules fixed before the replication. Luxar's self-calibrated budget
($\approx$\KstarRatio) keeps $\ge$97\% of nuclei under both detectors, but at a ratio where
JPEG-XL is also nearly
lossless ($\ge$\JXLworst{} up to \JXLmax) and far cheaper to compute. At hand-chosen
budgets of $\approx$\SI{200}{\times} and beyond, splats keep far more nuclei than JPEG2000
(Cellpose: \LuxHighCP{} vs.\ \JtkHighCP), until a cliff at $\lesssim$\CliffBelow{} splats
per nucleus that PSNR, flat within \LuxPSNRrange, does not reveal. Splat reconstructions
also cost the learned segmenter \GapRange{} points of recall relative to JPEG-XL at
\SIrange{45}{94}{\times} on every frame, while the blob detector is unaffected. Object-level
evaluation with the downstream detector should accompany Gaussian volume compression.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Light-sheet and confocal time-lapse microscopy produce terabytes per experiment, and
medical archives face the same pressure. Representing a volume as a set of anisotropic 3D
Gaussians \cite{kerbl2023gaussian} is attractive for such data: it is compact, continuous
and renders directly on the GPU. Recent systems fit Gaussian splats to microscopy volumes at
scale \cite{royer2026luxar}, to slice-based microscopy and ultrasound \cite{gaussianpile2026},
to medical volumes for compact storage \cite{gstoken2026,martin2026fixedbudget}, and to
tomographic reconstruction \cite{li2025threedgr,zha2024r2gaussian}. Quality is measured by
PSNR or SSIM throughout, and budgets are chosen by PSNR: Luxar selects its splat count by
self-supervised held-out PSNR \cite{batson2019noise2self} and describes its fits as intended
for visualisation rather than quantitative analysis \cite{royer2026luxar}.

What a microscopist needs from a compressed volume is that the analysis still works. Whether
a nucleus remains detectable hinges on a small, often faint peak. Radiology learned this for
lossy codecs, where acceptability is judged by whether lesions remain detectable, not by
PSNR \cite{koff2006overview}. Gaussian volume compression has not been held to that standard.

\textbf{Contributions.} (i)~A rate--detectability protocol: matched bytes on disk, dense human
labels, two detectors, one-to-one matching in physical units, and decision rules committed
before a replication on a second embryo. (ii)~Luxar's own budget is safe but sits where
JPEG-XL is equally safe; its advantage over codecs appears only at hand-chosen budgets
$\gtrsim$\SI{200}{\times}, bounded by a cliff that PSNR does not show. (iii)~Splat
compression lowers a learned segmenter's recall at ratios where JPEG-XL does not---a
detector dependence the codecs do not have.

\section{Related Work}
Gaussian mixtures have long modelled nuclei for segmentation and lineage tracking
\cite{amat2014fast}; our question concerns Gaussians as a \emph{storage} format. Gaussian
splatting \cite{kerbl2023gaussian} has been adapted to CT \cite{zha2024r2gaussian,li2025threedgr},
to slice-to-volume MRI with closed-form point-spread functions \cite{dannecker2026fast}, and
to volume compression \cite{royer2026luxar,gaussianpile2026,gstoken2026,martin2026fixedbudget},
each evaluated by image-fidelity metrics. JPEG2000 \cite{taubman2002jpeg2000}, JPEG-XL
\cite{alakuijala2019jpegxl} and the error-bounded ZFP \cite{lindstrom2014fixed} are the natural
baselines at matched size. We use Cell Tracking Challenge labels \cite{ulman2017objective,
maska2023cell} and two standard detectors: a scale-normalised Laplacian of Gaussian
\cite{lindeberg1998feature} and Cellpose \cite{stringer2021cellpose}.

\section{Methods}
\textbf{Data.} Fluo-N3DH-CE \cite{maska2023cell}: \emph{C.~elegans} embryos imaged at
$1.0\times0.09\times0.09\,\si{\micro\meter}$ (8-bit, $\approx$\SI{12}{MB} per frame), with
every nucleus labelled. We use embryo~1 (sequence 01) frames t150 and t194 and embryo~2
(sequence 02) frames t150 and t180, with \NlabEone{} and \NlabEtwo{} labelled nuclei; embryo~1
t100 (93 nuclei) enters only the budget analysis. Nuclei measure \SI{3.6}{\micro\meter}
(median over the challenge's segmentation masks); all spatial parameters are fixed
multiples of this diameter $D$.

\textbf{Compressors.} Luxar \cite{royer2026luxar} with default settings, 1000 iterations and
anisotropic voxel size, over seed budgets $K$ from $10^3$ to $6.4\times10^4$ (embryo~2:
$3.2\times10^4$); stored size is its \texttt{.gsplats.zarr} on disk. For embryo~1 we also run
Luxar's own calibration---blind-spot held-out PSNR \cite{batson2019noise2self}---over the same
$K$ grid to obtain its recommended $K^\ast$. JPEG2000 (3D, OpenJPEG), JPEG-XL (per slice), ZFP
fixed-rate and trilinear downsampling are bisected to each target size; sizes a codec cannot
reach are flagged and excluded from size-matched comparisons.

\textbf{Detectors and scoring.} (a)~LoG: local maxima in a physically isotropic window, ranked
by scale-normalised LoG response ($\sigma\in\{0.2,0.27,0.33,0.4\}D$); the top $N$ are kept,
$N$ = number of labelled nuclei. (b)~Cellpose~3 \emph{nuclei}, 2D per slice stitched across
$z$, at its own operating point. Detections match labels one-to-one within $0.6D$
(Table~\ref{tab:radius} varies it). \emph{Nuclei kept} = nuclei found after compression /
found in the raw volume by the same detector (1.0 = nothing lost). \emph{Survival}, a
label-free measure, is the fraction of the raw volume's 100 strongest LoG detections still
found.

\textbf{Decision rules.} Before the embryo-2 replication and the calibration runs, we committed
written pass/fail rules for each claim \todo{repository link}. One claim failed as worded and
is reported in its narrowed form below.

\textbf{Codecs.} JPEG-XL is nearly lossless for detection up to its maximum ratio
(\JXLmax; worst case \JXLworst{} under Cellpose). JPEG2000 degrades beyond
$\approx$\SI{100}{\times} and keeps \JtkHighRange{} of nuclei at $\approx$\SI{230}{\times}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:rd}, Table~\ref{tab:main}); downsampling and fixed-rate ZFP fail early.

\textbf{Luxar at its own budget.} Luxar's calibration recommends $K^\ast=\Kstar$ on both
embryo-1 frames---the top of our grid, with held-out PSNR still rising. There it keeps
\KstarLoG{} (LoG) and \KstarCP{} (Cellpose) of nuclei, at \KstarRatio. Its own rule is
therefore safe here, but it lands at ratios where JPEG-XL is equally safe, while fitting takes
\FitA{} per volume on an A100 (\FitH{} on an H100) against seconds for the codecs.

\textbf{Hand-chosen budgets.} Below $K^\ast$, splats reach ratios no codec matches. At
$\approx$\SI{230}{\times} Luxar keeps \LuxHighLoG{} (LoG) and \LuxHighCP{} (Cellpose) of
nuclei, against \JtkHighLoG{} and \JtkHighCP{} for JPEG2000, on both embryos. The advantage
is not uniform: at \SIrange{110}{135}{\times} on embryo~2, JPEG2000 at \SI{110}{\times} keeps
\RevJtkCP{} under Cellpose versus \RevLuxCP{} for Luxar, so our pre-registered claim
``Luxar leads beyond \SI{120}{\times}'' is narrowed to $\approx$\SI{200}{\times} and beyond.
Loss then sets in abruptly once splats become scarce (Fig.~\ref{fig:cliff}): on all four
frames Luxar drops below 90\% (LoG) only at $\le$\CliffBelow{} splats per labelled nucleus,
reaching \CliffOne{} at one per nucleus. Over the same budgets Luxar's PSNR moves only within
\LuxPSNRrange{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:psnr}); across all reconstructions PSNR orders \PSNRwrong{} of
pairs more than \SI{1}{dB} apart the wrong way round for faint nuclei kept.

\textbf{Detector dependence.} With the blob detector, Luxar keeps \LuxMidLoG{} of nuclei at
\SIrange{45}{94}{\times}; with Cellpose only \LuxMidCP, \GapRange{} points below JPEG-XL at
the same ratios, on all \GapFrames{} frames. The two detectors need very different budgets
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cliff}): the blob detector recovers every nucleus once there are $\approx$3--5
splats per nucleus, whereas Cellpose stays at 80--90\% from $\approx$5 to 50 splats per
nucleus and approaches the codecs only at $\approx$100 or more---the regime of Luxar's own
$K^\ast$. Covering each nucleus is not enough for a learned segmenter; the reconstruction
must also look like the images it was trained on (Fig.~\ref{fig:qual}). The deficit is
moderate relative to Cellpose's own variability---its scores move by up to \CPwobble{} points
between neighbouring sizes of the same codec---but consistent in sign across frames and
embryos, and the codecs show no comparable detector dependence.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
On this data, the case for Gaussian-splat \emph{storage} rests on ratios well beyond JPEG-XL's
reach, and there it depends on two things that image-fidelity metrics hide: the budget, and
the detector used afterwards. We recommend (i)~JPEG-XL wherever its ratio suffices; (ii)~for
higher ratios, checking a splat budget at the object level before trusting it (survival is a
label-free start); and (iii)~reporting rate--detectability with the detector that will be used
downstream.

Limitations: one organism (two embryos, five frames); Cellpose in its 2D-stitched mode and with
the variability noted above; a single Gaussian compressor at default settings; the
calibration evaluated on embryo~1 only; and larger early-embryo nuclei (t100) that fit our
fixed $D$ less well. Fine-tuning segmenters on splat reconstructions, and the same audit for
small low-contrast lesions in CT, are future work.

\section{Conclusion}
Measured at matched bytes against human labels, Gaussian-splat compression keeps nuclei at
ratios no standard codec reaches, but only above a budget that PSNR cannot identify, and with
a recall cost for learned segmenters that no image-fidelity metric exposes. Object-level,
detector-specific rate--detectability evaluation should accompany Gaussian volume compression.
