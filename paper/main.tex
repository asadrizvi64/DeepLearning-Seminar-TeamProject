\title{Do Gaussian Splats Keep the Nuclei?\\An Object-Level Audit of Gaussian Volume Compression for Fluorescence Microscopy}

\begin{abstract}
Gaussian splats are being adopted to store and stream large 3D microscopy and medical
volumes, with budgets chosen and quality reported by PSNR. We ask whether, at a given number
of bytes on disk, standard analysis still finds the nuclei. On four densely annotated frames
from two \emph{C.~elegans} embryos (\NlabTotal{} labelled nuclei), we compare a Gaussian-splat
compressor (Luxar) with JPEG2000 and JPEG-XL at identical stored bytes, scoring a blob
detector, a classical 3D watershed and a learned segmenter (Cellpose) against human labels,
with paired confidence intervals and decision rules fixed in advance. At every size from
$\approx$\KstarRatio{} to \JtkMaxRatio, a correctly configured JPEG2000 has higher PSNR and,
for both segmenters, keeps at least as many nuclei as the splats---significantly more in
\PairsJWatershed{} of \PairsNWatershed{} (watershed) and \PairsJCellpose{} of
\PairsNCellpose{} (Cellpose) size-matched pairs. Only the blob detector, which the splats'
smooth blobs suit, finds more nuclei in some splat reconstructions (by \LuxAdvPts{}
points at \LuxAdvRatio{} on one embryo, and on an early frame whose nuclei are larger). Below $\approx$2 splats per nucleus the splats lose
nuclei abruptly, while their PSNR stays within \LuxPSNRrange. Gaussian splats are not a
better storage format for this analysis than a standard wavelet codec; object-level,
size-matched evaluation against a verified codec baseline should accompany such claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Light-sheet and confocal time-lapse microscopy produce terabytes per experiment, and
medical archives face the same pressure. Representing a volume as a set of anisotropic 3D
Gaussians \cite{kerbl2023gaussian} is attractive for such data: it is compact, continuous
and renders directly on the GPU. Recent systems fit Gaussian splats to microscopy volumes at
scale \cite{royer2026luxar}, to slice-based microscopy and ultrasound \cite{gaussianpile2026},
to medical volumes for compact storage \cite{gstoken2026,martin2026fixedbudget}, and to
tomographic reconstruction \cite{li2025threedgr,zha2024r2gaussian}. Quality is measured by
PSNR or SSIM throughout, and budgets are chosen by PSNR: Luxar selects its splat count by
self-supervised held-out PSNR \cite{batson2019noise2self} and describes its fits as intended
for visualisation rather than quantitative analysis \cite{royer2026luxar}.

What a microscopist needs from a compressed volume is that the analysis still works. Whether
a nucleus remains detectable hinges on a small, often faint peak. Radiology learned this for
lossy codecs, where acceptability is judged by whether lesions remain detectable, not by
PSNR \cite{koff2006overview}. Gaussian volume compression has not been held to that standard,
nor compared with standard codecs at identical stored bytes.

\textbf{Contributions.} (i)~A rate--detectability protocol: identical bytes on disk, dense
human labels, three detector families, paired bootstrap intervals over nuclei, and decision
rules committed before each new measurement. (ii)~At identical bytes, JPEG2000 keeps at least
as many nuclei as Gaussian splats for both segmenters at every size we tested, and loses only
marginally to them under a blob detector at moderate ratios; Luxar's own budget is safe, but
so is the codec at the same bytes. (iii)~Splat reconstructions cost a learned segmenter
recall that blob detection does not reveal (a classical watershed loses less), and lose nuclei
abruptly below $\approx$2 splats per nucleus with no PSNR signal. (iv)~A baseline pitfall: a common JPEG2000
binding silently encodes a 3D array along the wrong axes, which had reversed our own
high-ratio conclusion.

\section{Related Work}
Gaussian mixtures have long modelled nuclei for segmentation and lineage tracking
\cite{amat2014fast}; our question concerns Gaussians as a \emph{storage} format. Gaussian
splatting \cite{kerbl2023gaussian} has been adapted to CT \cite{zha2024r2gaussian,li2025threedgr},
to slice-to-volume MRI with closed-form point-spread functions \cite{dannecker2026fast}, and
to volume compression \cite{royer2026luxar,gaussianpile2026,gstoken2026,martin2026fixedbudget},
each evaluated by image-fidelity metrics. JPEG2000 \cite{taubman2002jpeg2000} and JPEG-XL
\cite{alakuijala2019jpegxl} are the natural baselines at matched size. We use Cell Tracking
Challenge labels \cite{ulman2017objective,maska2023cell} and three detectors: a
scale-normalised Laplacian of Gaussian \cite{lindeberg1998feature}, a distance-transform
watershed, and Cellpose \cite{stringer2021cellpose}.

\section{Methods}
\textbf{Data.} Fluo-N3DH-CE \cite{maska2023cell}: \emph{C.~elegans} embryos imaged at
$1.0\times0.09\times0.09\,\si{\micro\meter}$ (8-bit, $\approx$\SI{12}{MB} per frame), with
every nucleus labelled. We use embryo~1 (sequence 01) frames t150 and t194 and embryo~2
(sequence 02) frames t150 and t180, with \NlabEone{} and \NlabEtwo{} labelled nuclei; embryo~1
t100 (93 nuclei) enters only the budget analysis. Nuclei measure \SI{3.6}{\micro\meter}
(median over the challenge's segmentation masks); all spatial parameters are fixed
multiples of this diameter $D$.

\textbf{Compressors.} Luxar \cite{royer2026luxar} with default settings, 1000 iterations and
anisotropic voxel size, over seed budgets $K$ from $10^3$ to $6.4\times10^4$ (embryo~2:
$3.2\times10^4$); stored size is its \texttt{.gsplats.zarr} on disk. For embryo~1 we also run
Luxar's own calibration---blind-spot held-out PSNR \cite{batson2019noise2self}---to obtain its
recommended $K^\ast$. JPEG2000 (OpenJPEG) codes each $xy$ slice with a 2D wavelet, all slices
as components of one codestream, and is bisected to \emph{exactly} the byte size of every
Luxar fit and to 50--800\,KiB; JPEG-XL codes each slice and is bisected to 50--800\,KiB.

\textbf{A baseline pitfall.} Passing a $(z,y,x)$ array to a common JPEG2000 binding encodes a
$z$--$y$ image with one component per $x$ column, so the wavelet never runs along $x$. That
configuration, which we used at first, costs $\approx$\SI{10}{dB} at \SI{220}{\times} and made
splats appear far better than JPEG2000 beyond \SI{200}{\times}. We now check the codestream
header (width, height, components) in a regression test.

\textbf{Detectors and scoring.} (a)~LoG: local maxima in a physically isotropic window, ranked
by scale-normalised LoG response ($\sigma\in\{0.2,0.27,0.33,0.4\}D$); the top $N$ are kept,
$N$ = number of labelled nuclei. (b)~Watershed: background-subtracted, Otsu-thresholded
foreground split by a distance-transform watershed in physical units; parameters fixed on
raw volumes only. (c)~Cellpose~3 \emph{nuclei}, 2D per slice stitched across $z$. On the raw
volumes they find \RecallLoG, \RecallWatershed{} and \RecallCellpose{} of the labelled nuclei.
Detections match labels one-to-one within $0.6D$ (Table~\ref{tab:radius} varies it).
\emph{Nuclei kept} = nuclei found after compression / found in the raw volume by the same
detector. Size-matched differences carry 95\% bootstrap intervals over labelled nuclei,
resampled jointly for the raw volume and both reconstructions.

\textbf{Decision rules.} Before each new measurement we committed written pass/fail rules
\todo{repository link}; claims that failed are reported in their narrowed form.

\textbf{Codecs.} JPEG-XL is nearly lossless for detection up to its maximum distance
(\JXLmax; worst case \JXLworst). JPEG2000 continues far beyond: at \FarRatio{} it still keeps
\FarJtkLoG{} (LoG), \FarJtkWatershed{} (watershed) and \FarJtkCellpose{} (Cellpose) of nuclei
(Fig.~\ref{fig:rd}).

\textbf{Splats vs.\ JPEG2000 at identical bytes.} Table~\ref{tab:main} pairs every Luxar fit
with the JPEG2000 encoding of the same size. For the segmenters, JPEG2000 keeps significantly
more nuclei in \PairsJWatershed{} of \PairsNWatershed{} pairs (watershed) and \PairsJCellpose{}
of \PairsNCellpose{} (Cellpose), and never fewer. Under the blob detector, Luxar keeps \LuxAdvPts{} points more at
\LuxAdvRatio{} on \LuxAdvFrames{} (its smooth blobs act as a denoiser for a blob filter; kept
exceeds 1), but beyond \SI{300}{\times} it keeps \FarLuxLoG{} against \FarJtkLoG{} for
JPEG2000. On E1 t100, whose early-stage nuclei are larger than our fixed $D$, the blob
detector's advantage on splats grows to \EarlyAdvPts{} points at \EarlyAdvRatio, while
neither segmenter favours Luxar in any pair there. JPEG2000 also has the higher PSNR in \PSNRjtkWins{} size-matched pairs.

\textbf{Luxar at its own budget.} Luxar's calibration recommends $K^\ast=\Kstar$ on both
embryo-1 frames---the top of our grid, with held-out PSNR still rising. There it keeps
\KstarLoG{} (LoG), \KstarWatershed{} (watershed) and \KstarCellpose{} (Cellpose) of nuclei, at
\KstarRatio; JPEG2000 at the same bytes keeps \KstarJtk{}, at the cost of seconds rather than
\FitA{} of fitting per volume on an A100 (\FitH{} on an H100).

\textbf{The budget cliff.} Splats lose nuclei abruptly once they become scarce
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cliff}): on all four frames Luxar drops below 90\% (LoG) only at
$\le$\CliffBelow{} splats per labelled nucleus, reaching \CliffOne{} at one per nucleus,
while its PSNR moves only within \LuxPSNRrange. Survival---the fraction of the raw volume's
100 strongest LoG detections still found, which needs no labels---tracks nuclei kept across
Luxar fits (Spearman $\rho=\SurvRho$, $n=\SurvN$) and can flag the cliff.

\textbf{Detector dependence.} At \SIrange{45}{94}{\times} the blob detector keeps
\LuxMidLoG{} of nuclei in Luxar reconstructions, while Cellpose keeps only \LuxMidCP{}:
\GapCP{} points below JPEG-XL at the same ratios on all \GapFramesCP{} frames (95\% intervals
exclude zero on \GapCIfrac). The classical watershed sits in between, at \LuxMidWS{} and
\GapWS{} points below JPEG-XL---consistently (intervals above zero on \GapWSCIfrac) but under
the 5-point threshold we fixed in advance on three of four frames. The large penalty is thus
specific to the learned segmenter: smooth blobs on a black background (Fig.~\ref{fig:qual})
lie outside the images it was trained on, whereas a blob filter is indifferent to them. Both
segmenters nonetheless favour JPEG2000 at identical bytes, and the watershed's deficit grows
with compression (Table~\ref{tab:main}). The blob detector recovers every nucleus once there
are $\approx$3--5 splats per nucleus; Cellpose needs tens to over a hundred, and on one frame
never reaches 90\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:cliff}). Cellpose's scores move by up to \CPwobble{} points between
neighbouring sizes of the same codec, so single comparisons are noisy, but the sign is
consistent across frames and embryos. Luxar itself is reproducible: three independent fits of
E1 t194 at each of three budgets differ by at most \RepLoG{} (LoG), \RepWatershed{} (watershed)
and \RepCellpose{} (Cellpose) points of nuclei kept.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
On this data, at identical stored bytes, Gaussian splats are not a better format for nuclear
analysis than a standard wavelet codec: JPEG-XL is nearly lossless to $\approx$\SI{100}{\times}
and JPEG2000 keeps at least as many nuclei, for both segmenters, at every larger ratio we
tested. Splats
remain attractive for what Luxar designs them for---continuous, GPU-native rendering---and a
blob detector tolerates them well. We recommend (i)~comparing Gaussian volume compression
with a verified codec baseline at identical bytes, (ii)~reporting object-level recall with
the detector that will be used downstream, and (iii)~checking a splat budget at the object
level before trusting it (survival is a label-free start).

Limitations: one organism (two embryos, five frames); Cellpose in its 2D-stitched mode; one
Gaussian compressor at default settings (tuned or entropy-coded splats may fare better); a
2D-per-slice JPEG2000 (a 3D wavelet would likely be stronger still); Luxar's recommended
budget scored on embryo~1 only (its calibration on embryo~2 also recommends
$K^\ast=\Kstar$); and larger early-embryo nuclei (t100) that fit our fixed $D$ less well. The same audit for small low-contrast lesions in CT is future work.

\section{Conclusion}
Measured at identical bytes against human labels, Gaussian-splat compression keeps nuclei
well at its own budget but no better than JPEG2000 at any budget for segmentation, loses
them abruptly below a budget PSNR cannot identify, and costs a learned segmenter recall that
a blob detector does not show. Object-level, size-matched evaluation against verified codec
baselines should accompany Gaussian volume compression.
