\title{Do Gaussian Splats Keep the Nuclei?\\An Object-Level Audit of Gaussian Volume Compression for Fluorescence Microscopy}

% Martin Weigert listed as supervising author -- confirm with him before submission.
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Syed Muhammad Asad, Akim Al-Makhdar, Martin Weigert}
\IEEEauthorblockA{TU Dresden, Dresden, Germany}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Gaussian splats are being adopted to store and stream large 3D microscopy and medical
volumes, with budgets chosen and quality reported by PSNR. We ask whether, at a given number
of bytes on disk, standard analysis still finds the nuclei. On \NFrames{} densely annotated
frames from two \emph{C.~elegans} embryos (\NlabTotal{} labelled nuclei), we pair each of
\NFits{} fits of a Gaussian-splat compressor (Luxar) with JPEG2000 encoded to exactly the
same bytes, and score four detectors against human labels: a blob detector, a classical 3D
watershed, and Cellpose in its 2D-stitched and 3D modes. PSNR favours JPEG2000 on every frame
and in all \PSNRjtkWins{} pairs. The detectors disagree: below $\approx$\SI{300}{\times}, 3D
Cellpose finds more nuclei in splat reconstructions (\FrModCPthree{} points, \FrModCPthreePos{}
of \FrN{} frames), the watershed and 2D Cellpose find more in JPEG2000 (\FrModWS{} and
\FrModCP{} points, all \FrN{} frames), and the blob detector shows no consistent difference.
The same network thus changes sides between its 2D and 3D modes. Beyond
$\approx$\SI{300}{\times}, at $\lesssim$3 splats per nucleus, splats lose nuclei abruptly and
every detector favours JPEG2000. PSNR is therefore not wrong but insufficient: it cannot tell
which representation is safer for a given analysis. Gaussian volume compression should be
evaluated at matched bytes, against a verified codec, with the detector that will be used.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Light-sheet and confocal time-lapse microscopy produce terabytes per experiment, and
medical archives face the same pressure. Representing a volume as a set of anisotropic 3D
Gaussians \cite{kerbl2023gaussian} is attractive for such data: it is compact, continuous
and renders directly on the GPU. Recent systems fit Gaussian splats to microscopy volumes at
scale \cite{royer2026luxar}, to slice-based microscopy and ultrasound \cite{gaussianpile2026},
to medical volumes for compact storage \cite{gstoken2026,martin2026fixedbudget}, and to
tomographic reconstruction \cite{li2025threedgr,zha2024r2gaussian}. Quality is measured by
PSNR or SSIM throughout, and budgets are chosen by PSNR: Luxar selects its splat count by
self-supervised held-out PSNR \cite{batson2019noise2self} and describes its fits as intended
for visualisation rather than quantitative analysis \cite{royer2026luxar}.

What a microscopist needs from a compressed volume is that the analysis still works. Whether
a nucleus remains detectable hinges on a small, often faint peak. Radiology learned this for
lossy codecs, where acceptability is judged by whether lesions remain detectable, not by
PSNR \cite{koff2006overview}. Gaussian volume compression has not been held to that standard,
nor compared with standard codecs at identical stored bytes.

\textbf{Contributions.} (i)~A rate--detectability protocol: identical bytes on disk, dense
human labels, four detectors, frame-level tests with paired intervals over nuclei, and
pass/fail rules committed before each new measurement, including a replication on eight
further frames. (ii)~PSNR picks the same winner at every size, but whether splats or a
standard codec keep more nuclei depends on the detector---and the same network changes sides
between its 2D and 3D modes. (iii)~A budget cliff at $\lesssim$3 splats per nucleus on every
frame, which PSNR does not reveal.
(iv)~A baseline pitfall: a common JPEG2000 binding silently encodes a 3D array along the
wrong axes, which had reversed our own high-ratio conclusion.

\section{Related Work}
Gaussian mixtures have long modelled nuclei for segmentation and lineage tracking
\cite{amat2014fast}; our question concerns Gaussians as a \emph{storage} format. Gaussian
splatting \cite{kerbl2023gaussian} has been adapted to CT \cite{zha2024r2gaussian,li2025threedgr},
to slice-to-volume MRI with closed-form point-spread functions \cite{dannecker2026fast}, and
to volume compression \cite{royer2026luxar,gaussianpile2026,gstoken2026,martin2026fixedbudget},
each evaluated by image-fidelity metrics. JPEG2000 \cite{taubman2002jpeg2000} and JPEG-XL
\cite{alakuijala2019jpegxl} are the natural baselines at matched size. We use Cell Tracking
Challenge labels \cite{ulman2017objective,maska2023cell}, a scale-normalised Laplacian of
Gaussian \cite{lindeberg1998feature}, a distance-transform watershed, and Cellpose
\cite{stringer2021cellpose}.

\section{Methods}
\textbf{Data.} Fluo-N3DH-CE \cite{maska2023cell}: \emph{C.~elegans} embryos imaged at
$1.0\times0.09\times0.09\,\si{\micro\meter}$ (8-bit, $\approx$\SI{12}{MB} per frame), every
nucleus labelled. We use six frames per embryo (t110--t194 of sequence 01, t110--t185 of
sequence 02; \NlabMin--\NlabMax{} nuclei each): four chosen first, eight added under rules
fixed in advance. Embryo~1 t100, whose early nuclei are larger, is reported separately.
Nuclei measure \SI{3.6}{\micro\meter}; all spatial parameters are fixed multiples of this
diameter $D$.

\textbf{Compressors.} Luxar \cite{royer2026luxar} with default settings, 1000 iterations and
anisotropic voxel size, at seed budgets $K$ from 500 to $6.4\times10^4$; its size is the
\texttt{.gsplats.zarr} on disk, metadata included. Renders are mapped to raw intensities by a
least-squares gain and offset (two numbers, not counted, which slightly favours Luxar). We also
run Luxar's own calibration---blind-spot held-out PSNR \cite{batson2019noise2self}---for its
recommended $K^\ast$. JPEG2000 (OpenJPEG) codes each $xy$ slice with a 2D wavelet, slices as
components of one codestream, bisected to \emph{exactly} the bytes of every Luxar fit and to
50--800\,KiB; JPEG-XL codes each slice, bisected to 50--800\,KiB.

\textbf{A baseline pitfall.} Passing a $(z,y,x)$ array to a common JPEG2000 binding encodes a
$z$--$y$ image with one component per $x$ column, so the wavelet never runs along $x$. That
configuration, which we used at first, costs $\approx$\SI{10}{dB} at \SI{220}{\times} and made
splats appear far better than JPEG2000 beyond \SI{200}{\times}; a regression test now checks
the codestream header.

\textbf{Detectors and scoring.} (a)~LoG: local maxima ranked by scale-normalised LoG response
($\sigma\in\{0.2,0.27,0.33,0.4\}D$), top $N$ kept, $N$ = number of labels. (b)~Watershed:
background-subtracted, Otsu-thresholded foreground split by a distance-transform watershed in
physical units, parameters fixed on raw volumes. (c)~Cellpose~3 \emph{nuclei}, 2D per slice
stitched across $z$, and (d)~the same model in its 3D mode (\FramesCPthree{} frames). On raw
volumes they find \RecallLoG, \RecallWS, \RecallCP{} and \RecallCPthree{} of the labelled
nuclei. Detections match labels one-to-one within $0.6D$ (Table~\ref{tab:radius} varies it).
\emph{Nuclei kept} = nuclei found after compression / found in the raw volume by the same
detector. Size-matched differences carry 95\% bootstrap intervals over labelled nuclei, resampled
jointly for raw and both reconstructions (Fig.~\ref{fig:matched}); they are per pair and not
corrected for multiple comparisons, so the claims rest on frame-level tests: frames as
independent units, Wilcoxon signed-rank, Holm-corrected (Table~\ref{tab:main}). Pass/fail rules were committed before each new
measurement (rules, code and results:
\url{https://github.com/asadrizvi64/DeepLearning-Seminar-TeamProject}); the two that failed are
reported in narrowed form.

\textbf{PSNR says JPEG2000; the detectors say it depends} (Table~\ref{tab:main},
Fig.~\ref{fig:matched}). We treat the \FrN{} frames as independent units and test the mean
per-frame difference with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Holm-corrected over the eight
detector--regime comparisons. PSNR is higher for JPEG2000 on every frame in both regimes
and in all \PSNRjtkWins{} pairs (by \DPSNR). Below \SI{300}{\times}, 3D Cellpose favours the
splats (Luxar $-$ JPEG2000 = \FrModCPthree{} points of nuclei kept, \FrModCPthreePos{} of
\FrN{} frames, $p_\mathrm{Holm}=\FrModCPthreeP$), whereas the watershed and 2D Cellpose favour
JPEG2000 (\FrModWS{} and \FrModCP{} points, every frame, $p_\mathrm{Holm}=\FrModWSP$ and
\FrModCPP). The blob detector shows no consistent difference (\FrModLoG{} points,
\FrModLoGPos{} of \FrN{} frames, $p_\mathrm{Holm}=\FrModLoGP$): splats help it on embryo~2
(\FrModLoGETwo) but not embryo~1 (\FrModLoGEOne). Beyond \SI{300}{\times} (up to
\JtkMaxRatio) every detector favours JPEG2000 on \FrFarNeg{} of \FrN{} frames
($p_\mathrm{Holm}\le\FrFarLoGP$), by \FrFarLoG{} (LoG) to \FrFarCP{} (2D Cellpose) points;
JPEG2000 still keeps \FarJtkLoG{} (LoG) and \FarJtkWS{} (watershed) of nuclei there. PSNR is
thus right at extreme ratios and for slice-wise segmentation, and wrong for 3D Cellpose at
moderate ratios. JPEG-XL is nearly lossless up to its maximum distance (\JXLmax; worst case
\JXLworst).

\textbf{Why the detector matters.} Smooth splats can act as a denoiser for detectors that pool
over a volume: under LoG and 3D Cellpose nuclei kept often exceeds 1 (Luxar at
\SIrange{45}{94}{\times}: \LuxMidLoG{} and \LuxMidCPthree). Per-slice and threshold-based
segmenters instead see smooth blobs on a black background with streaks (Fig.~\ref{fig:qual}).
The same Cellpose network shows this cleanly. At \SIrange{45}{94}{\times}, JPEG-XL minus
splats is \GapCP{} points of nuclei kept in 2D mode (intervals above zero on \GapCIfrac{}
frames) but \GapCPthree{} in 3D mode, where the 5-point penalty we tested for in advance
appeared on \RuleGapBigCPthree{} frames---the penalty belongs to slice-wise processing, not to
the learned model. The watershed sits in between (\GapWS{} points; under the pre-set 5 points
on \RuleGapSmallWS{} frames).

\textbf{The budget cliff and PSNR.} Splats lose nuclei abruptly once they become scarce
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cliff}): under LoG, nuclei kept crosses 90\% at \CrossRange{} splats per
labelled nucleus on all \NFrames{} frames, as pre-registered ($\le$3.5). Over budgets
$K\ge1000$, Luxar's PSNR varies by at most \LuxPSNRspan{} per frame (\LuxPSNRrange{} overall).
Our pre-registered test that PSNR misses the cliff within $K$ = 1000--16\,000 held on only
\RulePsnrOK{} added frames: on smaller frames the cliff lies below that range. Survival---the fraction
of the raw volume's 100 strongest LoG detections still found, which needs no labels---tracks
nuclei kept across fits (Spearman $\rho=\SurvRho$, $n=\SurvN$) and can flag the cliff.

\textbf{Luxar at its own budget.} Luxar's calibration recommends $K^\ast=\Kstar$ on all
\KstarCalFrames{} calibrated frames of both embryos---the top of our grid, held-out PSNR still
rising. There, at \KstarRatio, it keeps \KstarLoG{} (LoG), \KstarWS{} (watershed), \KstarCP{}
(2D) and \KstarCPthree{} (3D Cellpose) of nuclei; JPEG2000 at the same bytes keeps
\KstarJtk, in seconds rather than \FitA{} of fitting per volume on an A100 (\FitH{} on an H100).

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Three independent Luxar fits of E1 t194 at each of three budgets
differ by at most \RepLoG{} (LoG), \RepWS{} (watershed) and \RepCP{} (2D Cellpose) points;
Cellpose's own scores move by up to \CPwobble{} points between neighbouring codec sizes. On
E1 t100, with larger early nuclei, the blob detector favours splats more strongly
(\EarlyAdvPts{} points at \EarlyAdvRatio).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
On this data no storage format is best for every analysis. JPEG-XL is nearly lossless to
$\approx$\SI{100}{\times}; beyond it, JPEG2000 kept more nuclei for slice-wise and
threshold-based segmentation, and for every detector we tested past \SI{300}{\times}, while
Gaussian splats helped volumetric Cellpose at moderate ratios, at the cost of minutes of GPU
fitting. None of this establishes that any format is safe in general. PSNR is not
random---its preference for JPEG2000 matched most of these outcomes---but it ranks the two
formats the same way at every size, so it is insufficient to determine detector-specific
outcomes. We recommend comparing
Gaussian volume compression with a verified codec at identical bytes, reporting object-level
recall with the detector that will be used downstream, and checking a splat budget at the
object level before trusting it (survival is a label-free start).

Limitations: one organism (two embryos, 12 frames); one Gaussian compressor at default
settings (tuned or entropy-coded splats may fare better); a 2D-per-slice JPEG2000 (a 3D wavelet
would likely be stronger still); frames, not embryos, as the units of replication (two
embryos); and a fixed $D$ that suits early, larger nuclei (t100) less well. The same audit for
small low-contrast lesions in CT is future work.

\section{Conclusion}
Measured at identical bytes against human labels, Gaussian-splat compression keeps nuclei
well at its own budget, gains or loses nuclei against JPEG2000 at moderate ratios depending
on the detector---even between the 2D and 3D modes of one network---and loses them abruptly
below a budget that PSNR cannot identify.
Object-level, size-matched, detector-specific evaluation should accompany Gaussian volume
compression.
